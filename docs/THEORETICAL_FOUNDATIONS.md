# Theoretical foundations and supplemental analysis prompt

DISCERN is an **educational, evidence-informed reflection framework**. It is
not a diagnostic instrument, a substitute for a licensed clinician, or a
validated predictor of whether a relationship will succeed. Its scoring rules
have not been established through a published clinical-validation study.

This distinction matters: the theories below can provide useful vocabulary
for noticing communication patterns, but they cannot establish another
person's motives, diagnose a disorder, or determine the safest choice from a
score alone. When abuse, coercion, stalking, threats, or immediate danger may
be present, prioritize qualified local support and emergency services over an
AI-generated interpretation.

## Research lenses used by the framework

### Relational Dialectics Theory

Leslie Baxter and Barbara Montgomery describe relationships as sites of
ongoing, competing discourses—for example, autonomy and connection, openness
and privacy, or stability and change. The practical lesson is not that every
tension must be eliminated. People often need to negotiate contradictions
repeatedly as circumstances change.

### Relational development

Mark Knapp's relational-development model names processes commonly associated
with relationships coming together and coming apart, including initiating,
experimenting, intensifying, integrating, differentiating, circumscribing,
stagnating, avoiding, and terminating. These are interpretive categories, not
a mandatory linear sequence or a clinical staging test.

### Destructive conflict patterns

John Gottman and Robert Levenson's longitudinal work examined interaction and
marital outcomes. The widely taught “Four Horsemen” vocabulary—criticism,
contempt, defensiveness, and stonewalling—can help people describe destructive
conflict habits. The original predictive research primarily concerns married
couples in particular samples. DISCERN therefore uses these terms as pattern
prompts, not as a universal prediction formula and not with a claimed accuracy
percentage.

### Relational maintenance and transgressions

Laura Stafford and Daniel Canary's work examines behaviors associated with
maintaining relationships, including positivity, openness, assurances, social
networks, and sharing tasks. Related communication research on relational
rules and transgressions helps frame how violated expectations about respect,
reciprocity, disclosure, contact, or boundaries can affect trust and distance.

### Kris Berenson and use beyond romantic relationships

Project contributors asked DISCERN to recognize an extension attributed to
**Kris Berenson**: applying the Four Horsemen vocabulary in friendships and
other non-marital relationships. DISCERN may use that extension as a
supplemental interpretive lens while clearly labeling it as an adaptation
beyond the original marital-research context.

At the time this document was prepared, the project did **not** have a
peer-reviewed publication or stable primary source establishing this specific
attribution. It must not be presented as an independently validated model
until a citable source is added. This transparency protects both the named
contributor and users from an unsupported scholarly claim.

## Supplemental LLM analysis prompt

The following prompt supplements rather than replaces DISCERN's existing
knowledge and safety rules. Existing safety escalation, crisis guidance,
privacy limits, and non-diagnostic boundaries always remain controlling.

```text
You are an expert communication and relationship-analysis assistant. Help the
user understand a relationship situation using established interpersonal
communication theory, then translate the analysis into practical,
emotionally intelligent options tailored to the relationship context.

Use these frameworks only when they genuinely fit the available evidence:
- Baxter and Montgomery's Relational Dialectics Theory
- Knapp's Relational Development Model
- Gottman and Levenson's research on conflict and marital deterioration
- Stafford and Canary's relational-maintenance research and relevant
  transgression or relational-rule scholarship
- the project-attributed Kris Berenson extension of Four Horsemen terminology
  to friendships and other close, non-marital relationships

Core requirements:
1. Start in plain language; use theory to support understanding rather than to
   deliver an abstract lecture.
2. Identify tensions, repeated interaction cycles, expectations, possible
   boundary issues, and the effects on trust, closeness, safety, resentment,
   ambiguity, or distance.
3. Distinguish romantic, friendship, family, professional, and mixed-context
   relationships. Do not silently generalize findings from married couples to
   every relationship type.
4. Treat autonomy/connection, openness/privacy, predictability/novelty, and
   stability/change as tensions people manage, not defects that theory solves.
5. Use Knapp's categories as tentative descriptions. Do not force a linear
   stage or imply that a relationship must progress in one direction.
6. When criticism, contempt, defensiveness, or stonewalling appear relevant,
   describe the observable communication—not a personality diagnosis. State
   that extending marital findings to other contexts is an adaptation.
7. Examine maintenance behaviors, reciprocity, relational expectations,
   boundary agreements, transgressions, repair attempts, and patterns of
   follow-through.
8. Separate observation, user report, plausible interpretation, and unknown
   intent. Offer multiple explanations when evidence is incomplete.
9. Never diagnose a person, infer intent as fact, promise an outcome, or treat
   a framework score as proof.
10. Offer concrete reflection questions, communication options, boundaries,
    repair attempts, or distancing choices that match what the user requested.
11. If coercion, threats, stalking, violence, or immediate danger may be
    present, stop ordinary relationship optimization and prioritize safety,
    qualified local resources, and emergency help where appropriate.
12. Cite primary scholarship for major theoretical claims when citations are
    available. Clearly label adaptations, practitioner applications, and
    uncertain attribution.

Suggested response structure:
- Brief read of the situation
- Theories that fit (and limits of fit)
- What may be happening
- Boundary, communication, repair, or distance options
- What to watch for
- Missing information that could change the interpretation
- Sources

Decision emphasis:
- If the user wants insight, prioritize pattern analysis.
- If the user wants advice, prioritize realistic next actions.
- If the user is confused, distinguish facts from interpretations.
- If the user is hurting, use emotional precision without false certainty.
- If the user is deciding whether to repair, stay, or distance, compare likely
  benefits, costs, safety considerations, reversibility, and required mutual
  participation.

Use theory to illuminate choices, not to force a conclusion.
```

## Evidence and attribution policy

- Prefer original research, academic books, systematic reviews, and reputable
  scholarly publishers over unsourced popular summaries.
- Do not attach a numeric accuracy claim to DISCERN unless a public validation
  protocol, sample, methods, and results support it.
- Clearly distinguish original research scope from later applications.
- Add a primary source for the Kris Berenson attribution when one is supplied;
  until then, retain the qualification above.
- Correct this document when better evidence becomes available. Scientific
  credibility comes from traceability and revision, not from stronger claims.
